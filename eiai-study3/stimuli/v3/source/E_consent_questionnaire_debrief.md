## E1. Information and consent

Welcome! This short study is part of a student research project at Nazarbayev Intellectual School of Science and Mathematics, Kokshetau. We are testing an AI tutor designed for a space-training learning game, and we want to understand how well people learn with it and use what they learn.

What you will do: complete a short training in three modules with an AI tutor called Nova, handle a few mission situations, and fill out a short questionnaire. It takes about 20 minutes.

Who can take part: people aged 18 or older who are comfortable reading English (B1 level or above).

Your data: participation is anonymous. We do not collect your name, email or other contact details. Your answers are stored securely and used only for research. You can stop at any time by closing the page, and at the end you can ask for your data to be removed.

Risks and benefits: there are no known risks beyond everyday computer use. At the end you will learn more about the study and see your mission readiness score.

Some details of the study will be explained only at the end, so that they don't influence your answers.

Questions: dashaprokop24@gmail.com. This study was reviewed by the Local Research Ethics Committee of Nazarbayev Intellectual School of Science and Mathematics, Kokshetau (positive decision of 14 September 2026, valid until 14 September 2027).

**What this study was really about**
We are studying how people evaluate information from AI assistants. Two things varied in this study:

1. Nova's style. Some participants met a warm, emotionally supportive Nova; others met a neutral, matter-of-fact Nova. You met the {warm | neutral} version.
2. The quality of Nova's explanations. Every fact Nova taught you is true. But for half of the facts, Nova's explanation was solid: it gave evidence that actually supports the fact. For the other half, the explanation was flawed: it used true but irrelevant details, weak comparisons or other reasoning that does not actually support the fact. Which facts got which kind of explanation was decided at random.

We want to know whether a warm, supportive tone changes how carefully people check the reasons behind what an AI tells them. We could not tell you this at the start, because knowing it would have changed how you read the explanations.
